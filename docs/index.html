Public Release, October 2026

H1: The Hidden-Identity
Linking Benchmark

Across a patient's report mentions, does a system keep one continuing finding together, and keep two look-alike findings with the same key apart?

What it is. 400 synthetic worlds. Each world holds 8 to 14 mentions of findings across 3 to 5 reports. A system groups the mentions. A scorer compares the groups with the groups the authors wrote.

What it is not. The worlds are synthetic. The inputs are structured frame slots (oracle frames), not raw report text. The benchmark tests linking only, not extraction. No clinician labelled anything, and the labels encode the scenario the authors wrote. This is not clinical validation.

Ranking caveat. The team that wrote the benchmark also wrote the FindingFrame linker. Its results appear below as author reference baselines and carry no rank.

The Task

A patient has several reports over time. A report may mention the same liver lesion in March and again in June, with different wording. Another report may mention a second lesion at the same site. A tracking system must decide which mentions belong together.

H1 asks for two groupings of every world's mentions:

  • Category partition. Mentions of the same finding type, organ, and side go together.
  • Entity partition. Mentions of the same individual lesion go together. Two lesions can share one category, so entities are finer than categories.
Plain scope. The inputs are frame slots (finding type, anatomy, laterality, measurement, change, assertion), each with an opaque mention ID. The benchmark gives no patient ID, category ID, or entity ID. The scorer holds the labels, and they come from the authored scenario. They are not clinician judgments.

How It Works

The system never sees a label. The scorer reports category and entity quality separately, so a system cannot hide a weak entity result behind a strong category result.

The eight v2 families

The test split has 400 worlds, 50 per family. Each world holds a target category and one or two look-alike distractor categories.

  • granularity_with_laterality_distractor. Rib or bone metastasis on one side, with the same finding on the other side. The target's own mentions switch between "rib" and "bone".
  • granularity_with_organ_distractor. Rib or bone metastasis on one side, with a sideless spine or bone metastasis sharing the word "bone".
  • laterality_pair_same_type. Lung metastasis on one side and on the other side.
  • same_word_different_organ. A breast primary tumor, a stomach primary tumor, and a liver metastasis, all described with "mass" or "lesion".
  • same_site_different_type. Lung metastasis and pneumonia or infection in the same lung, same side.
  • simultaneous_distinct_with_distractor. Two liver metastases at once (same key, two entities), plus an abdominal lymph node metastasis.
  • descriptor_pair_with_distractor. Two breast primary tumors with the same key, described with or without a distinguishing descriptor, plus a rib bone metastasis.
  • episode_reuse_with_distractor. A liver metastasis resolves, then a new lesion reuses the same key, plus a rib bone metastasis.

Source: DATA_CARD.md and the contracts/ folder in the repository.

Metrics

  • Category B-cubed F1 and Entity B-cubed F1 are co-primary. We never combine them into one score.
  • Split and merge rates. The share of true same-group pairs the system separated (split), and the share of true different-group pairs it joined (merge). Each is reported for categories and for entities.
  • Coverage. The unresolved rate: the share of mentions a system left without a group. Unresolved mentions stay in the denominator.
  • Uncertainty. Every interval is a 95% CI from a world bootstrap (10,000 draws, seed 42). Paired contrasts resample within each family and weight families equally.
Why v2 exists. In v1, every test world held one gold category. A system that put every mention of a world in one group scored a category F1 of 1.000, and a category merge error could not happen. We found this on 2026-09-27, while reviewing an interim look at v1 results. v2 puts 2 to 3 categories in every world, so merging distinct findings now costs as much as splitting one. Read v1 results only with this flaw in mind. We keep v1 in the repository, marked as flawed. Only v1 train and dev worlds ship. There is no v1 test split in the repository. Every v1 test world had one gold category. In the shipped v1 dev data, two families hold more than one: laterality_change (two categories in all 100 worlds) and report_order_permutations (two in 51 of 100).

In v2 the same one-group baseline scores a category F1 of 0.676, and the one-group-per-mention baseline scores 0.344. Trivial baselines sit beside every model result below for this reason.

Results on the v2 Test Split

Read this before using the scores.
  • Simple rules solve the Category axis. The strict-laterality ablation scores 1.000, and so does a rule written by hand from the public contract.
  • Entity is the axis that separates systems.
  • That hand-written rule reached Entity F1 0.955 on the private test labels. This is above every published row (the best is 0.927, the strict-laterality ablation).
  • Treat v2 as a diagnostic of linking behaviour. It is not a leaderboard.
  • Raw-text v3 is planned to remove this ceiling.

All rows use the same 400 test worlds. GLM answered 342 of them. GLM spent its whole token budget on reasoning in all three tries for the other 58. Those worlds stay in the denominator and score as unresolved singletons. This earns partial credit, not zero: mean category F1 on the 58 failed GLM worlds is 0.326. What the numbers support

  • Neither model beats the compatibility linker on categories. Category F1 is 0.925 for DeepSeek and 0.850 for GLM, against 0.947 for the linker. Both paired differences are negative and both intervals exclude zero.
  • Both models reduce same-key entity merges. Entity merge is 0.088 (DeepSeek) and 0.089 (GLM) on their entity partitions, against 0.176 for exact key and for the compatibility linker.
  • The merge gain does not become an entity F1 gain for GLM. The 58 unanswered worlds count as unresolved mentions, which lowers its entity F1 relative to exact key. DeepSeek's entity partition does beat exact key on entity F1.

Scope note: these are results for two models, one prompt, temperature 0, on synthetic oracle-frame worlds. Each world got one run, except that GLM had up to three attempts per world. They say nothing about extraction from real reports.

Run It Yourself

A clean machine can install the package, run a smoke test, score a model on the development set, and build a test submission. Each step is one command.

git clone https://github.com/Sher110106/findingframe-h1 && cd findingframe-h1
python -m venv .venv && source .venv/bin/activate
pip install -e ".[openai,test]"
pytest -q
h1bench smoke
h1bench baselines --split dev

export OPENAI_API_KEY=...
h1bench run --split dev --base-url https://api.openai.com/v1 --model gpt-4o-mini
h1bench score --split dev predictions_dev_gpt-4o-mini.jsonl

h1bench run --split test --base-url https://api.openai.com/v1 --model gpt-4o-mini
h1bench make-submission predictions_test_gpt-4o-mini.jsonl --model "gpt-4o-mini"

h1bench run calls any OpenAI-compatible endpoint and writes a predictions file named predictions_<split>_<model>.jsonl (change it with --out). h1bench score scores the development set locally, because its labels are public. h1bench make-submission checks the test predictions and writes submission.json.

The repository README lists the full options for each command.

Submitting a Model

Test labels stay private. If they were public, systems could tune to the test set. The maintainer scores test submissions instead.

  • Open a pull request that adds submissions/<team>-<model>/submission.json.
  • The maintainer checks the schema and scores the file privately with the frozen test labels.
  • The maintainer posts aggregate and per-family scores. Per-world scores stay private.
  • Each model version gets one scored submission every 7 days.
What stays private. Test labels, per-world scores and predictions, generator code and seeds, and the FindingFrame production modules. The exact-key, compatibility, strict-laterality, and descriptor baselines therefore appear as results only.

Limits and Roadmap

Stated limits.
  • The inputs are oracle frames. A system never has to read report text, so H1 cannot say how a system performs on real reports.
  • The worlds are synthetic and authored. The labels encode the authors' intent, and no clinician checked them.
  • The same team wrote the benchmark and the FindingFrame linker. That is why its rows carry no rank.
  • Six of the eight families are solvable on categories from the raw slots alone. In 300 of 400 test worlds, grouping by the exact slot tuple matches the gold categories. The two granularity families, and the entity axis, carry the harder tests.
  • One of the two LLMs failed to answer 58 worlds. GLM's unresolved mention rate by family: 0.36 in granularity_with_laterality_distractor, 0.32 in granularity_with_organ_distractor, 0.22 in same_site_different_type, 0.18 in laterality_pair_same_type, 0.06 in episode_reuse_with_distractor, 0.02 in simultaneous_distinct_with_distractor, and 0.00 in the other two.
  • v3 (next). Worlds will render report text. Families and renderer styles will be held out. The linker stays unranked.
  • H1-U (later). Uncertainty and abstention: a system may decline to link, and the scorer will report coverage and calibration apart from both F1 scores.

We promise no dates.
